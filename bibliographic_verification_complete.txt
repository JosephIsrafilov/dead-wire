POTTER 1870 VERIFICATION — COMPLETED WITH CRITICAL FINDING
================================================================================

Source: "The Telegraph Instructor: A Rudimentary Treatise on the Art of
Telegraphing" by Arthur Potter
Publisher: Flemming, Potter & Co., Philadelphia, 1870
Library of Congress Control No. 08000715
PDF Retrieved: Yes (2.8 MB)

VERIFIED PAGE REFERENCES:

1. MORSE ALPHABET SECTION: Page 7
   Footer text: "THE ART OF TELEGRAPHING. 7"

   Verified content:
   - Complete alphabet table with dots and dashes
   - Spaced letters identified: c, o, r, y, z
   - Quote: "Some letters are called spaced letters, as c, o, r, y, and z,
     and known thus: (two dots, space, dot, for c,) (dot, space, dot, o,)"
   - "The letter l is represented by a dash twice the length of t"

2. SPACING RULES SECTION: Pages 7-8
   Section heading: "SPACING."

   Verified spacing rules:
   - Intra-letter gap: "count one"
   - Inter-letter gap: "count three"
   - Spaced-letter internal gap: "count two"
   - Long dash (L): "count eight"
   - Inter-word gap: "count four"

3. TIMING RATIO — CRITICAL DISCREPANCY
   Potter 1870 page 7 states:

   "To make a dot, strike with a hammer as you count one and raise it
   instantly; to make a dash, strike and count four, then raise again.
   The dash taking four times as long as the dot."

   This explicitly describes:
   - Dot = 1 count
   - Dash = 4 counts
   - Ratio = 1:4 (NOT 1:3)

CRITICAL CONFLICT:

The audit document Section 3.1 claims:
  "Short Dash Mark: 3 units"
  "Primary Citation: Potter 1870, p. 13"

Potter 1870 page 7 actually states:
  "The dash taking four times as long as the dot"

This is a 1:4 ratio, not the 1:3 ratio the audit attributes to Potter.

RECOMMENDATIONS:
1. Update all Potter page references from p.13-14 to p.7-8
2. Address timing ratio discrepancy in Section 3.2
3. Clarify bibliographic source for 1:3 ratio (likely Pope, not Potter)

POTTER 1870 VERIFIED QUOTES FOR AUDIT REFERENCE
================================================================================

Alphabet (page 7):
"The Morse Alphabet, as used in this country for telegraphing, is made up
of the following combination of signs, called dots and dashes"

Spaced letters (page 7):
"Some letters are called spaced letters, as c, o, r, y, and z, and known
thus: (two dots, space, dot, for c,) (dot, space, dot, o,)"

Long dash (page 7-8):
"The letter L may be prolonged to count eight and called long dash"

Timing ratio (page 7):
"To make a dot, strike with a hammer as you count one and raise it instantly;
to make a dash, strike and count four, then raise again. The dash taking
four times as long as the dot."

Spacing rules (page 7-8):
- "The space between the parts of a letter (except in spaced letters,)
   must be while you count one"
- "The space between different letters in the same word should be while
   you count three"
- "In spaced letters count two for the space"
- "Spaces between words last while counting four"
