You are Elias Crane, a professional telegraph operator working the night shift in an isolated railroad office. You know railroad procedure. You know Morse better than the player does.

The signal on the wire is objective. It never lies, and the game never secretly scrambles it to cheat you. But the man transcribing it — you — is another matter.

The game will never print “Elias made an error.” The mismatch is yours to notice. What you file as the official record becomes the truth the world keeps.

The Story

A town that drank the mine,
and a company that rewrote the record.

October 14, 1889. Mining operations at Black Creek broke into a dangerous underground water source. The railroad company's own infrastructure distributed it through town. A neurological illness spread. The company suppressed what it knew.

Five years later Elias Crane returns to the town where his family became part of the evidence chain. His mother was quietly removed to a private institution in Denver. His father — a physician — died in a remote company location. A letter and an altered telegram are still out there.

By day, Elias walks the ruined town, reads documents, cross-checks records, and talks to what remains of the disaster. By night, he works the wire — and slowly stops being able to trust the man holding the pen.

Every mistake becomes part of the world. There is no GAME OVER — only a record that gets harder to correct.

Design Pillars

Horror made of information.

No weapons. No monsters chasing you down a corridor. The pressure is procedural, spatial, and psychological.

nº I
·— · —·

Morse Integrity

The true signal exists independently of Elias — a source-of-truth pipeline the game must never secretly rewrite. Learn enough American Morse and you can verify any message yourself. The game may distort the man. It may not distort the wire.

nº II
·· · ·—

Divided Attention

Sounder, transcript, ledger, routing board, door, window — the office geometry guarantees you cannot watch them all at once. Is it uncomfortable to turn away from the window when the sounder starts clicking? That discomfort is the game.

nº III
—· · ·—·

Consequence Without Reload

Mistakes become state, not fail screens. A wrong filing closes a clue, raises suspicion, or surfaces days later. The optimal strategy is never “reload until perfect” — it's living with an imperfect record.

nº IV
···· · ·—

Sparse Horror

First comes a believable, boring night shift you learn by heart. Only then do small wrongnesses start earning their fear. The unusual must remain unusual — no jumpscare economy, no haunted-every-minute.

The Office

One room, built like a machine.

Every object has a mechanical purpose. The tension is in the geometry before anything strange ever happens.
